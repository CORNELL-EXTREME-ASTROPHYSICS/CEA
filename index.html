Listening for the extreme universe.

We study the most energetic phenomena in the cosmos — merging supermassive black holes, ultra-dense pulsars, and millisecond flashes from across the universe — using the largest radio telescopes on Earth as our instruments.

Hellings–Downs correlation

γ(θ)

The angular signature a gravitational-wave background imprints on timing residuals across a pulsar timing array — first predicted by Hellings & Downs in 1983, and the pattern NANOGrav reported evidence for in 2023.

    What we study

    Four windows on the extreme universe

    From nanohertz gravitational waves to millisecond radio flashes, our group turns radio telescopes into some of the most sensitive instruments in physics.

    Gravitational Waves & PTAs

    Timing an array of millisecond pulsars to hunt for nanohertz gravitational waves from supermassive black hole binaries.

    Read more →

    Fast Radio Bursts

    Localizing millisecond-duration flashes from distant galaxies to trace the universe’s missing baryons and magnetized plasma.

    Read more →

    Radio Instrumentation

    Building the backends, arrays, and survey pipelines — from Arecibo’s legacy to the Deep Synoptic Array — that make these detections possible.
